# Domestic Deployment Accountability Act

## Subtitle

Domestic Deployment as Constitutional Boundary — Open-Source Model Legislation

## What is this?

This is open-source model legislation establishing accountability requirements for domestic deployment of the Armed Forces, federalized National Guard, or other military force within the United States.

## Why should I care?

Domestic military deployment changes the boundary between civil authority, military force, state governance, and the person. This model legislation gives lawmakers, advocates, researchers, policy staff, and civic organizations a concrete framework for requiring verified predicates, judicial review, record preservation, time limits, oversight, and public reporting.

## What is the claim?

The central claim is that domestic military deployment should be fact-based, proportionate, time-limited, record-preserving, and reviewable. Military force within the United States should not be used without a valid statutory predicate, and protected civic activity should not be converted into an emergency identity.

## What is this not?

This is not enacted law, legal advice, or a complete operational military-law framework. It is a public civic/legal model for domestic deployment accountability and constitutional boundary preservation.

## Who is this for?

This artifact is for lawmakers, legislative staff, civil-rights advocates, state and local officials, public-law researchers, journalists, civic organizations, students, and members of the public interested in domestic deployment limits and accountable emergency authority.

## How was this done?

The model statute uses a Verified Threat Predicate structure, 72-hour judicial review, CACR record retention, automatic termination and renewal rules, independent inspectorate review, enforcement provisions, congressional review, public reporting, and a civil-authority / Posse Comitatus preservation rule.
